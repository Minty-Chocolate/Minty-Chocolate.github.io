## Paper Review: Executive Summary & Thesis

Applying deep learning to systematic statistical arbitrage often degrades into an unconstrained "black-box price prediction" exercise that 
ignores decades of structural quantitative finance. *Attention Factors for Statistical Arbitrage* stands out because it respects structural discipline: 
instead of using raw neural nets to predict unconstrained price changes, it reformulates the classical Barra multi-factor risk architecture via a 
self-attention mechanism.

The paper offers a compelling mathematical bridge: it casts factor portfolio construction and systematic residualization as a unified dual optimization 
problem solved end-to-end. While the empirical PnL profiles illustrate familiar macro headwinds, the methodological innovation of learning dynamic linear 
factor spaces via transformers sets a solid blueprint for modern equity market-neutral pipelines.

## Mathematical Framework: Modernizing the Barra Paradigm

### The Classical Structural Baseline

In standard cross-sectional risk modeling (e.g., Barra), asset returns $r_t \in \mathbb{R}^N$ decompose into systematic factor risk and idiosyncratic 
residual returns:

$$r_t = B_t f_t + \epsilon_t$$

where:

* $B_t \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times K}$ is the factor exposure/loading matrix ($K \ll N$).
* $f_t \in \mathbb{R}^K$ is the vector of unobserved factor returns.
* $\epsilon_t \in \mathbb{R}^N$ represents idiosyncratic residuals intended for mean-reverting StatArb capture (e.g., via OU processes).

Under ordinary least squares (OLS) cross-sectional regression, assuming asset weights or generalized least squares weighting matrix $W_t \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$, 
the estimated factor return is:

$$f_t = (B_t^\top W_t B_t)^{-1} B_t^\top W_t r_t = F_t^\top r_t$$

Here, $F_t \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times K}$ denotes the factor-mimicking portfolio weights.

### The Exposure-Weight Duality & The Residualizer Matrix

The core theoretical elegance of this paper lies in exploiting the **primal-dual relation** between factor exposures $B_t$ and factor portfolio weights $F_t$.

By construction, factor-mimicking portfolios must satisfy the exact exposure constraint:

$$B_t^\top F_t = I_K$$

Consequently, the projection (hat) matrix that maps total returns to systematic factor-explained returns is:

$$P_t = B_t (B_t^\top B_t)^{-1} B_t^\top = B_t F_t^\top \quad \text{(when orthogonalized)}$$

The idiosyncratic returns are isolated directly using the orthogonal residualizer matrix $M_t$:

$$\epsilon_t = (I_N - P_t) r_t = M_t r_t$$

where $M_t \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$ satisfies idempotency ($M_t^2 = M_t$) and projectivity ($M_t B_t = 0$). In practice, the inversion is not unique,
so a ridge penalty is enacted.


## End-to-End Differentiability

The core engineering achievement of the framework is replacing the traditional, disjointed two-step statistical arbitrage pipeline with a fully differentiable 
computational graph. 

The architecture achieves this through several smoothly connected, differentiable modules:


* **Attention-Based Factor Weights:** Instead of relying on non-differentiable heuristic sorting or static PCA eigenvector extraction, the factor portfolio weights 
$\omega_{t-1}^F$ are generated through a scaled dot-product attention mechanism. Firm characteristics are first mapped to embeddings ${X}_{t-1} = X_{t-1} W^K$, 
and then attended to by learnable query vectors $Q$ representing latent factors. The weights are normalized using a row-wise Softmax function:


$$\omega_{t-1}^F = \text{Softmax}\left(\frac{Q {X}_{t-1}^T}{\sqrt{d}}\right)$$


This operation is entirely smooth and allows gradients to flow directly back to the underlying firm characteristics.


![](/images/writing/attention-factors/pic1.png)


* **LongConv Sequence Modeling:** The extracted residual returns are fed into a LongConv sequence model to predict arbitrage weights: 
$\omega_{t-1}^{\text{port}} = \text{LongConv}_\theta(\epsilon_{(t-s, t-1)})$. This layer bypasses the exploding/vanishing gradients of recurrent networks 
by utilizing the FFT convolution theorem for $O(T \log T)$ complexity. Additionally, the convolution kernel $\mathcal{K}$ is regularized using a 
sub-differentiable element-wise Squash operator:


$$\mathcal{K} = \text{sign}(\mathcal{K}) \max(\vert{}\mathcal{K}\vert{} - \lambda_{\text{squash}}, 0)$$



This acts as a proximal step for an $L_1$ penalty, smoothly shrinking small kernel weights to zero.


* **Friction-Aware Dual Objective:** The network's loss function jointly optimizes explained variance and the strategy's net Sharpe ratio after transaction 
costs. The net portfolio return explicitly deducts an $L_1$ transaction cost ($5$ basis points) and a shorting cost ($1$ basis point):



$$R_{t, \text{net}}^{\text{port}} = R_t^{\text{port}} - \left( 0.0005 \times \Vert{}\omega_t - \omega_{t-1}\Vert{}_1 + 0.0001 \times \Vert{}\max(-\omega_t, 0)\Vert{}_1 \right)$$


While the absolute value ($L_1$ norm) and the $\max$ operator are not continuously differentiable at zero, they are *sub-differentiable*. 
This allows the model to continuously penalize high-turnover churn during training, fundamentally shaping the upstream attention factors to 
discover signals that survive real-world execution.


## Empirical Results

A methodological issue in reporting the paper's performance I'd take is the choice of AUM curve rather than cumlog returns standard equity log-wealth curves.
The pnl visuals brush over the fact that stat arb efficiency have undergone significant shifts, examplified by the decay post GFC and the Covid shock. Furthermore,
funds are moving away from factor premia and incorporating more forward information than ever. The proposed architecture is insightful and elegant, but to further 
address the underperformance in recent years, a valuable step to take would be to explicitly expand the residualization pipeline to strip out forward-looking alpha 
proxies in addition to existing backward-looking risk factors before executing the mean-reversion loop.
